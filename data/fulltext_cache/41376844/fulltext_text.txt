The global escalation of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) poses an urgent threat to public health and modern clinical practice, undermining the efficacy of existing antibiotic therapies and increasing the prevalence of persistent and multidrug-resistant infections (Yakobi and Nwodo, 2025a; Yakobi et al., 2024a). Conventional antibiotics primarily target intracellular metabolic pathways (Yakobi and Nwodo, 2025b), however, widespread resistance driven by efflux pumps, biofilm formation, membrane remodelling, and horizontal gene transfer has dramatically reduced their long-term clinical utility (Yakobi et al., 2025; Elfadadny et al., 2024). In response, antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) have emerged as promising alternatives due to their broad-spectrum activity, rapid bactericidal mechanisms, and reduced propensity for resistance development (Agüero-Chapin et al., 2022; Saubenova et al., 2025). AMPs exert their effects primarily through direct interactions with microbial membranes or surface-exposed molecular targets, making them particularly relevant against pathogens that resist conventional therapeutics (Saubenova et al., 2025; Capecchi et al., 2021). Among host-defense peptides, LL-37, HNP-1, and magainin-2 represent mechanistically distinct yet archetypal AMP classes. LL-37, the only human cathelicidin, is released from the hCAP-18 precursor during inflammatory activation and displays pronounced structural plasticity, transitioning from disordered to amphipathic α-helical conformations upon contact with lipid bilayers (Ridyard and Overhage, 2021). This conformational adaptability enables LL-37 to disrupt diverse bacterial membrane environments through carpet-like dissolution and immunomodulatory signalling roles (Szymczak and Szczurek, 2023). In contrast, HNP-1, an α-defensin expressed by neutrophils, adopts a rigid three-stranded β-sheet stabilized by three conserved disulfide bonds. Its structural rigidity supports pore assembly and lipid clustering rather than dynamic membrane dissolution (Johnson and Deber, 2020). Magainin-2, derived from Xenopus laevis, forms a stable amphipathic α-helix that inserts into membranes to induce toroidal pore formation—a well-defined mechanism of membrane permeabilization with minimal off-target cytotoxicity (Hirano et al., 2021). Despite extensive functional characterization, the structure–function relationships that define the mechanistic distinctions among these peptides remain incompletely understood at the level of molecular dynamics, target engagement, and allosteric modulation (Capecchi et al., 2021; Bae et al., 2025). Moreover, AMPs increasingly demonstrate activities beyond direct membrane disruption, including efflux pump modulation, outer membrane porin destabilization, enzyme inhibition, and immunoregulatory signalling (Denardi et al., 2021). These expanded functional roles emphasize the need for computationally resolved, mechanistically explicit modelling approaches that integrate secondary structure prediction, physicochemical profiling, docking, and dynamic conformational analysis. In this study, we unify how three archetypal AMPs convert fold topology and amphipathic architecture into distinct dynamic outcomes on clinically relevant membrane-associated targets (MexB, LpxC, OprF), establishing mechanism-level design rules that can be used to engineer next-generation membrane-active therapeutics with reduced resistance susceptibility. By integrating physicochemical parameters, structural confidence metrics, and dynamic correlation patterns, we delineate the distinct mechanistic signatures that differentiate adaptive carpet-like dissolution (LL-37), disulfide-rigidified pore stacking (HNP-1), and toroidal pore initiation (magainin-2). This work provides a unified comparative framework linking AMP structural organization to functional mechanisms, offering actionable insights for rational AMP optimization, therapeutic peptide design, and strategic targeting of membrane-associated resistance determinants. To assess mechanistic similarities and differences between LL-37, HNP-1, and magainin-2, we compared six structural and functional descriptor metrics, including docking affinity (−HADDOCK score), consensus AMP likelihood, structural model quality (ERRAT score, and Ramachandran favoured percentage), charge state at physiological pH, and amphipathic hydrophobic moment (Fig. 13). The resulting multivariate performance profiles revealed three distinct mechanistic signatures. Magainin-2 demonstrated uniformly high structural convergence and antimicrobial confidence, exhibiting the highest Ramachandran favoured residue proportion (100 %), near-perfect model quality (ERRAT = 100), and a high AMP consensus score (0.98). This profile is consistent with its pre-organized amphipathic α-helical conformation and toroidal pore formation mechanism. HNP-1 displayed a similarly high AMP consensus score (0.98), but with lower Ramachandran convergence (70.8 %), reflecting the intrinsic backbone constraints imposed by its disulfide-stabilized β-sheet scaffold. Despite this, HNP-1 recorded strong docking affinity and high ERRAT quality, consistent with its rigid pore-stacking membrane disruption mechanism. LL-37 showed a distinct functional profile characterized by the highest hydrophobic moment and greatest net positive charge, features that enable adaptive carpet-like membrane dissolution and curvature matching. However, LL-37 exhibited lower AMP consensus probability (0.76) and moderately reduced structural organization, aligning with its intrinsically disordered-to-helix transition behaviour and environment-dependent effector function. Despite the growing interest in AMPs as alternatives to conventional antibiotics, the precise structural and mechanistic bases that differentiate how individual AMPs disrupt bacterial membranes or modulate membrane-associated targets remain poorly defined (Wan et al., 2024). This lack of mechanistic clarity limits rational peptide optimization, clinical translation, and the design of resistance-resilient therapeutics (Agüero-Chapin et al., 2022; Bae et al., 2025). LL-37, HNP-1, and magainin-2 are widely studied, mechanistically distinct AMPs that represent three major functional classes of membrane-interacting peptides (Ridyard and Overhage, 2021; Johnson and Deber, 2020; Hirano et al., 2021; Denardi et al., 2021). By comparing their maturation pathways, structural organization, amphipathic properties, protein-target docking behaviour, and dynamic interaction effects, this will establish a unified mechanistic framework linking peptide structure to antimicrobial action (Li et al., 2022). Clarifying these relationships is essential for guiding rational AMP engineering, optimizing therapeutic performance, and reducing the likelihood of resistance development. In this study provides a comparative structural and mechanistic resolution of three archetypal AMPs—LL-37, HNP-1, and magainin-2—highlighting how differences in biosynthetic maturation, amphipathic organization, and conformational flexibility translate into distinct membrane-interaction and target-modulation behaviours. By integrating consensus secondary-structure prediction, structural modelling, physicochemical profiling, machine-learning classification, protein–peptide docking, and normal-mode dynamics analysis, we delineate three fundamentally different strategies for antimicrobial action, adaptive carpet-like dissolution, rigid pore-stacking lipid reorganization, and toroidal pore formation. The maturation pathways identified through SignalP situate each peptide within its biological context. LL-37, which lacks a canonical signal peptide and is released proteolytically during inflammation, aligns with a role as a context-dependent immunomodulatory effector rather than a constitutively stored antimicrobial agent. In contrast, the Sec/SPI-directed maturation of HNP-1 and magainin-2 supports their evolutionary function as pre-formed, rapidly deployable membrane-targeting effectors. This divergence in biosynthetic regulation parallels the distinct biophysical architectures observed in secondary-structure analyses. Our structural predictions show experimentally consistent fold classes, a long amphipathic α-helix for LL-37, a rigid disulfide-stabilized β-sheet for HNP-1, and a short but stable α-helix for magainin-2. Notably, the plasticity of LL-37, indicated by moderate pLDDT scores and terminal disorder, contrasts sharply with the high structural order of magainin-2 and the cysteine-locked rigidity of HNP-1. This difference proved consequential in downstream interaction modelling and dynamic analyses (Wan et al., 2024). Amphipathic profiling further distinguished the peptides' modes of membrane engagement. LL-37 exhibited the highest hydrophobic moment and net positive charge, features that support curvature matching and surface spreading characteristic of carpet-like bilayer dissolution. Magainin-2 displayed a stable but narrower amphipathic helical core, consistent with toroidal pore nucleation, while HNP-1's absence of helical amphipathicity underscores its β-sheet-mediated stacking and lipid clustering mechanism. These physicochemical signatures were mirrored in the machine-learning classification scores, where HNP-1 and magainin-2 both achieved consensus AMP likelihoods of 0.98, while LL-37 scored lower (0.76), reflecting its greater functional conditionality. Protein–peptide docking further demonstrated that LL-37 does not form a single stable binding pose, but samples multiple shallow surface-associated states along the MexB vestibule. Normal-mode analysis indicated that this interaction dampens porter-domain mobility, consistent with allosteric rigidification that disrupts the efflux cycle rather than channel blockage. This provides mechanistic support for LL-37's potential role as a non-competitive efflux pump modulator, a mechanism increasingly recognized but previously under-characterized at the dynamic level. HNP-1 bound to LpxC with a reproducible β–acidic-groove interface, stabilizing catalytic loops and decreasing conformational flexibility. This corresponds to entropic inhibition through dynamic locking, whereby peptide binding constrains active-site reorganization rather than directly occluding the catalytic pocket. Such a mechanism allows enzymatic suppression without requiring direct metal-chelation or substrate mimicry, expanding the targetability of LpxC-like enzymes (Leone et al., 2023). Magainin-2 displayed a third mode of target interaction, its helical axis aligned laterally along the lumen-facing arc of OprF, enhancing correlated β-barrel breathing motions. The increase in cooperative fluctuations, together with negative ΔΔG and positive ΔΔS values, suggests that magainin-2 lowers the energetic barrier for toroidal pore initiation, preparing the membrane for curvature-dependent rupture. The multivariate clustering and PCA analysis further illustrated these distinctions, positioning HNP-1 and magainin-2 within a shared rigid pore-forming class, while LL-37 occupied a structurally flexible, context-responsive functional space. This separation offers a framework for rational peptide engineering, increasing structural plasticity that may enhance environmental adaptability, whereas increasing amphipathic convergence may stabilize pore-forming efficacy (Courtney et al., 2022). These findings highlight that AMP activity is not defined solely by membrane affinity or charge, but by the specific dynamic and mechanical consequences of peptide engagement with protein or membrane targets. The relationship between structure, dynamics, and antimicrobial mechanism revealed here provides a mechanistic basis for designing next-generation AMPs with enhanced potency, reduced cytotoxicity, and minimized resistance susceptibility.
