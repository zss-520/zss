Bioactive peptides are usually small molecular peptides [1], which are a class of peptides with multiple biological functions and widely exist in animals, plants and foods [2]. As bioactive peptides play an important role in regulating various functions of the body, peptide drugs also have the characteristics of significant curative effect, short half-life and low toxicity [3]. In recent years, more and more bioactive peptides with different functions have been found [4–6]. At the same time, more and more experimental data show that many bioactive peptides have multiple functions. For example, antimicrobial peptides (AMPs) can protect the host from pathogen invasion [7], while some AMPs also have other functions, such as antibacterial, antifungal, antiviral and anticancer [8]. Antiangiogenic peptides (AAPs) are used in the field of cancer treatment because of their function of reducing or inhibiting angiogenesis [5, 9], and they also play an important role in the treatment of other angiogenesis-dependent diseases [10–12]. Some antioxidant peptides (AOPs) extracted from natural proteins may contain additional bioactive properties, such as cholesterol-lowering or antimicrobial properties, in addition to their own antioxidant functions [3, 13]. Accurately predicting the functional activity of bioactive peptides has various meanings: (1) it helps to study the mechanism of action of existing bioactive peptides; (2) it helps to discover new bioactive peptides and to search for bioactive peptides with specific functions; and (3) it helps to develop new peptide drugs to meet the needs of health and safety. Because bioactive peptides have a wide range of application prospects, it is particularly important to realize the method of large-scale identification of bioactive peptides. Traditional experimental methods cannot meet the needs of peptide function identification, and the accurate identification of bioactive peptides by computational methods has become more and more important. There are quite a few existing methods that can be used to identify bioactive peptides. In 2023, Lee et al. [14] proposed antibacterial peptide function prediction based on the Bert model, named AMP-BERT, which has a bidirectional encoder representation (BERT) architecture from the transformer [15]. In 2022, Pang et al. [16] proposed a method combining transformer neural network architecture and natural language processing knowledge to identify AMP and AMP's functional activity. In 2023, Xu et al. [17] proposed a framework of iAMPCN based on deep learning. The model is mainly based on four types of sequence features and can predict AMP and its functional activity. In 2021, Chen et al. [18] proposed to improve the prediction ability of anti-cancer peptides (ACPs) by using a data augmentation method, called ACP-DA, which could improve the prediction performance of the model while solving the problem of insufficient sample size. In 2022, Bhattarai et al. [19] improved the features used based on Chen [18] to further improve the accuracy of predicting ACPs. In 2019, Zahiri et al. [20] proposed AntAngioCOOL to predict AAPs. They extracted useful description information from different aspects such as PseAAC, atomic spectra and physicochemical spectra to build models. Additionally, another methods [21–24] are usually only applicable to the functional identification of a single bioactive peptide, and only two methods can perform multi-functional identification of bioactive peptides related to the human. One is a deep learning method called MLBP proposed by Tang et al. [25] in 2022 based on convolutional neural networks (CNN) [26] and gated recurrent units (GRU) [27], and the other is a multi-activity prediction method, called MPMABP, for bioactive peptides based on CNN and Bi-LSTM [28] proposed by Li et al. [29] in 2022. Compared to MLBP, MPMABP has been improved by constructing a residual network to ensure that the information of peptides is not lost and replacing GRU with LSTM. However, MPMABP used the same dataset as MLBP, where the variety of bioactive peptides did not increase and did not solve the problem of data imbalance. The identification of bioactive peptides with multiple functions is a multi-label classification problem, so multi-label classification methods from text, image, and bioinformatics fields over the past few decades can also be used to identify bioactive peptides [30–36]. In 2008, a multi-label learning method, named CLR, proposed by Johannes et al. [33] introduced an artificial calibration label, which is a method that combines paired preference learning with traditional correlation classification techniques. In 2018, a novel cost-sensitive multi-label learning model, named CPNL, with positive and negative label pair correlation proposed by Wu et al. [31] solves the problem that binary correlation (BR) ignores label correlation. In 2020, another method combining ranking support vector machine and binary correlation with robust low-rank learning, named RBRL, proposed by Wu et al. [34] inherits the ranking loss minimization advantage of ranking support vector machine (Rank-SVM) and overcomes the problem of BR class imbalance and ignoring the problem of label correlation. In 2019, the multi-label deep forest method, named MLDF, proposed by Zhou et al. [35] includes two important parts: measuring perceptual feature reuse and measuring perceptual layer growth. In 2007, Grigorios et al. [36] proposed a multi-label classification method called RAKEL that uses a single-label classifier to consider label correlation. However, among the multiple methods mentioned above, the multi-label classification method that can be applied to multiple tasks has limited predictive power when used alone for the task of multi-functional identification of bioactive peptides, and the accuracy of identification is also affected by zero-padding of peptide sequences, which cannot provide comprehensive and accurate functional identification. MLBP and MPMABP use peptide sequences as the only input information, and their accuracy decreases significantly when faced with the identification of more categories of peptide functions. Given these limitations, we propose a new method BPFun for identifying bioactive peptides. For feature construction, we extracted features from multiple perspectives to capture the relationship between peptide sequence information and function. We selected five different feature representation methods as inputs: peptide sequence, peptide sequence encoded by one-hot, peptide sequence encoded by AAindex1 [37], and peptide sequence features obtained using pre-trained models ProtT5 [38] and ESM-2 [39]. The construction process of the BPFun model can be summarized as follows: (1) Obtain and organize a dataset of bioactive peptides and preprocess it, which includes seven bioactive peptides: AMP, ACP, ADP, AHP, AIP, AAP, and AOP; (2) Using AAindex1 and pre-trained models ProtT5 and ESM-2 to process peptide sequences to obtain new encoding and features; (3) Replace the amino acid letters in the peptide sequence with natural numbers to obtain the preprocessed peptide sequence and use one-hot encoding; (4) Using deep learning models to construct BPFun models; (5) Evaluate the performance of the model. Use nine metrics to evaluate the performance of BPFun. Finally, the performance evaluation structure indicates that under the combined action of these five different features, we have effectively improved the representation of peptide sequences. Compared with existing prediction tools, our BPFun has excellent performance in identifying multi-functional peptides and outperforms MLBP and MPMABP in the results of seven functional classifications. Based on the principle of being able to identify more bioactive peptides, we used nine bioactive peptides related to humans retrieved from the 2020 Google Scholar engine provided by Tang et al. [25], and selected seven of them, including AMP, ACP, ADP, AHP, AIP, AAP, and AOP, with a sample size of more than 100. Among these seven bioactive peptides, the five bioactive peptides AMP, ACP, ADP, AHP, and AIP used the same experimental dataset as MLBP [25] and MPMABP [29], the AAP dataset used the same experimental dataset as AntAngioCOOL [20], and the AOP used the same dataset as AnOxPePred [40]. These datasets were screened using the clustering tool CD-HIT [41] with a threshold set to 0.9. In the experiment, we used five different types of encoding methods to represent peptide sequences. To ensure the efficiency of our process of obtaining these five sequence representations, we analyzed the length distribution of all data. In the results of length distribution, 99.3% of the sequence length was less than 128, 92.1% of the sequence length was less than 64, and 79% of the sequence length was less than 32. Therefore, we selected the peptide sequence length range from 5 to 32. Sequences with a length less than 5 will be deleted, sequences with a length greater than 32 will be truncated from the first 32 amino acids, and sequences with a length less than 32 will be filled using the zero-padding method, to ensure that there is sufficient sequence information without filling in too many zeros, which will affect data quality. One-hot encoding can visually represent amino acid sequences by converting each amino acid into a vector consisting of 0 and 1. The composition of each amino acid is unique. However, one-hot encoding ignores the sequence information between amino acids, that is, it does not take into account the relative positional relationship between amino acids. Therefore, we need to use other feature information together. AAindex [37] is a database representing numerical indices of various physicochemical and biochemical properties of amino acids and pairs of amino acids. The AAindex1 database contains statistical indices of the physicochemical properties of 544 amino acids, covering aspects such as hydrophilicity, molecular weight, and molecular volume. We can use AAindex1 encoding to integrate these rich physicochemical property information into the amino acid sequence. We replace the amino acids of a peptide sequence and finally obtain a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$32 \times 544$$\end{document} numerical matrix. Unlike one-hot encoding, AAindex1 encoding can more accurately reflect the physicochemical properties and biological features of the amino acid sequence. The ProtT5 [38] is a model that trains a large number of protein sequences in a self-supervised manner and is only applicable to amino acids with uppercase letters. The features extracted from this self-supervised model (LM embedding) capture important biophysical properties that control protein shape. The structure is shown in Fig. 1. Each amino acid in the amino acid sequence is mapped to a fixed length vector representation through an embedding layer, and position encoding is introduced to encode the relative position of each amino acid in the sequence. Then, the stacking of Transformer encoders is used to capture the relationships in the sequence and generate higher-level representations, thereby obtaining feature information. ProtT5 not only focuses on the sequence itself, but also on the relationship between the sequence and its structure. Therefore, we chose the ProtT5-XL-U50 pre-trained model to extract features of peptide sequences. The ProtT5 [38] is a model that trains a large number of protein sequences in a self-supervised manner and is only applicable to amino acids with uppercase letters. Afterward, the output information from the upper layer will enter the multi-head self-attention layer [43], where the main idea of the multi-head self-attention mechanism is to learn multiple attention in different representation spaces to capture different levels and aspects of information. We input the integrated feature information into the multi-head self-attention layer and perform linear transformation through three matrices to obtain three different matrices: query matrix (Q), key matrix (K), and value matrix (V), all of which have the same model dimension. Then, for each head, perform matrix multiplication on Q, K, and V to obtain the score matrix. Due to the scarcity of bioactive peptides that can determine their functional activity, and the irregular composition of amino acids, accurately predicting the functional activity of bioactive peptides remains challenging. A single feature vector is not sufficient to effectively provide the demand for predicting bioactive peptides. Therefore, to improve the performance of the model in predicting bioactive peptides, we used different features to represent the same bioactive peptide, to fully utilize the information of a bioactive peptide sequence. Through visual analysis of features, it can be concluded that using the five features we have selected simultaneously is far more suitable for predicting the function of bioactive peptides than using only one feature alone. Therefore, we propose a prediction method for multi-functional bioactive peptides, called BPFun, which uses multiple feature information to predict bioactive peptides. From our experimental results, it can be seen that this method has good comprehensive performance. Compared with existing methods, BPFun has better performance in predicting bioactive peptides. We believe that the following reasons have improved the performance of BPFun. First, we use different features to represent peptide sequences. We used five different features, including the original sequence, to describe sequence information from different perspectives. In the experiment, we made heat maps for different categories of bioactive peptides and different feature representations, and the results showed that these five different features can effectively represent the information of peptide sequences. Compared to using a single feature as input, our approach of using different features can effectively improve model performance. Among the existing methods for identifying bioactive peptides, using multiple different features can achieve more significant improvements in experiments compared to using a single feature method [18, 19]. Second, we use data augmentation to address the issue of data imbalance between different categories, especially after the number of bioactive peptide categories has increased to seven. Data augmentation is the process of generating pseudo samples by modifying the original sequence: randomly replacing a certain amino acid letter in the sequence with a specific character, and then obtaining a new sequence. The dataset obtained through data augmentation can train the model more effectively compared to the dataset without data augmentation. The model's predictive ability for categories with smaller sample sizes is significantly improved, and the sensitivity of these categories with smaller sample sizes can be significantly improved. Finally, we optimized the model. Using convolutional layers of different scales to extract different features can effectively utilize the different features we use. The use of Bi-LSTM can effectively utilize the contextual information of peptide sequences. Meanwhile, the attention mechanism used in the model effectively improves the performance of the model. Under the same dataset conditions, our method has better performance and overall performance compared to other methods. In summary, our proposed method BPFun has good performance in predicting the function of bioactive peptides. The different optimization methods used can effectively improve the performance of the model. Among them, using multiple different representation methods to describe sequence information and data augmentation of samples can effectively alleviate the problem of insufficient and insufficient effective information in bioactive peptide data. Accurately identifying bioactive peptides and their functional activities is crucial for the design of functional peptides and the development of therapeutic approaches. In this work, we propose a deep neural network based BPFun for predicting the functional activity of multi-functional bioactive peptides. In this model, five different feature representation methods including sequence information were used, and the data was expanded. Then, features were extracted using CNNs of different scales, and attention mechanisms were added to process information. The prediction results of the BPFun method indicate that compared with existing bioinformatics multi-label methods and deep learning methods, the BPFun method is relatively better, or at least can supplement future computing models in this field. In the future, we will continue to study the direction of multiple feature information working together, consider more efficient feature extraction and combination methods and different algorithms, and try to select representative feature information with other feature information; we will also focus on solving the problem of data imbalance to further improve the prediction performance of multifunctional bioactive peptides.
