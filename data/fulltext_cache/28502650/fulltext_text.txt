Eriocheir sinensis, also known as the river crab or hairy crab, is an economically important freshwater species in China with significant ecological and economic value. As market demand continues to grow, its aquaculture scale has been expanding. According to statistics, the farming production of E. sinensis reached 888,629 tons in 2023. Then the 14-3-3 proteins are recognized to play an important role in pathogen transmission and infection processes. Meanwhile, protein phosphorylation, as one of the most common post-translational modifications, can alter the physicochemical properties of proteins, thereby affecting their spatial structure and regulating physiological and biochemical processes such as neural activity and cell proliferation. Through phosphorylation-dependent interactions, 14-3-3 proteins occupy a central position in signaling pathways, dynamically regulating host growth, development, and stress responses. However, their role during pathogen infection in crabs remains unclear. First, we employed proteomic analysis combined with dsRNA-mediated interference to preliminarily investigate the role of E. sinensis 14-3-3ζ (Es14-3-3ζ) in the host immune process. Subsequently, phosphoproteomic analysis along with a phospho-tyrosine specific antibody was utilized to validate the Phosphoinositide-3 kinase (PI3K)-mediated phosphorylation of Es14-3-3ζ. To explore the functional impact of this phosphorylation modification, a site-directed mutagenesis assay was conducted in the Drosophila S2 cell line. In vitro experiments primarily included assessments of cell viability, apoptosis, and the copy number of Spiroplasma eriocheiris (S. eriocheiris). His-pull down assays were performed to screen for proteins interacting with Es14-3-3ζ, and co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) as well as cellular co-localization experiments were further employed to confirm these protein-protein interactions. Finally, the intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels following phosphorylation modification and miRNA-mediated silencing were measured using an ROS detection kit. Previous proteomic analysis revealed Es14-3-3ζ was significantly reduced during intracellular pathogen S. eriocheiris infection. RNAi of Es14-3-3ζ enhanced the S. eriocheiris infection and death of E. sinensis. The phosphorylation level of Es14-3-3ζ at the conserved tyrosine residue (Tyr129) was significantly reduced during the early stage of S. eriocheiris infection. The Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation at Tyr129 significantly enhanced cell viability, reduced apoptosis, and decreased S. eriocheiris load in Drosophila S2 cells. PI3K, Thioredoxin (TRX1), and catalase (CAT) were identified as interacting partners of Es14-3-3ζ. Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation promoted protein-protein interactions. Overexpression of EsPI3K led to a significant increase in Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation, whereas RNAi of EsPI3K resulted in reduced phosphorylation. Furthermore, Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation significantly decreased TRX1 and CAT activity to elevate the ROS levels. Finally, miR-2309 was identified as targeting Es14-3-3ζ. Based on our previous proteomic analysis, Es14-3-3ζ expression was significantly downregulated to 0.679-fold after S. eriocheiris infection 10 d, suggesting its potential role in the immune defense mechanisms of E. sinensis [31–33]. Multiple alignments of 14-3-3ζ with other species showed that 14-3-3ζ shared 97.98%, 97.57%, 89.07%, and 89.47% with 14-3-3s from Palaemon carinicauda, Scylla paramamosain, Bombus terrestris, and Helicoverpa armigera, respectively (Fig. S1A). The recombinant Es14-3-3ζ was expressed in E. coli with an apparent molecular weight of around 28 kDa. After recombinant Es14-3-3ζ was purified by affinity chromatography (Fig. S1C and S1D), the rabbit antiserum was prepared and also reactive with a constituent that had an apparent molecular weight 28 kDa (Fig. S1E). The result of qRT-PCR shown that Es14-3-3ζ was transcribed in all tissues of E. sinensis including nerve, intestine, muscle, gill, heart, hemocytes and hepatopancreas (Fig. 1A) and transcribed highest in the intestine, followed by the muscle and nerves. During S. eriocheiris infection, Es14-3-3ζ mRNA levels in hemocytes up-regulated significantly at the early phase of 2 d post infection (dpi), returned to the normal level after 4 dpi and down-regulated significantly at 10 dpi (Fig. 1B). Similar to the qRT-PCR, western blot analysis showed that Es14-3-3ζ expression in hemocytes increased at the 2 dpi, then decreased after 6 dpi (Fig. 1C). After stimulated in vitro with S. eriocheiris, the expression of Es14-3-3ζ in primary cultured E. sinensis hemocytes was increased after 12 and 24 h post infection (hpi) (Fig. 1D). And the immunofluorescence analysis shown there was a significant increase in Es14-3-3ζ expression in hemocytes at 12 and 24 hpi, confirming the involvement of Es14-3-3ζ in the S. eriocheiris infection (Fig. S2A and S2B). After individual-level RNA interference (RNAi) of the Es14-3-3ζ with dsRNA, the interference efficiency was assessed using qRT-PCR and Western blot after 48, 72, and 96 h interference (the interference primers were shown in supplementary Table S1). Compared with the control group injected with GFP-dsRNA, the transcription level of Es14-3-3ζ in the Es14-3-3ζ-dsRNA group decreased by 35%, 51%, and 52% at 48, 72, and 96 h, respectively (Fig. 1E), while, the expression levels decreased by 39%, 38%, and 28%, respectively (Fig. 1F and G). These findings indicate that Es14-3-3ζ could be interfered by dsRNA. After Es14-3-3ζ RNAi, the crabs were infected with S. eriocheiris, the S. eriocheiris load in the Es14-3-3ζ-dsRNA group was significantly higher than that in the control group from 2 dpi, indicating that the silencing of the Es14-3-3ζ facilitated the proliferation of S. eriocheiris in the crab hemocytes (Fig. 1H). Subsequent survival rate analysis yielded results consistent with the copy number of S. eriocheiris. From 3 dpi onward, the survival rate of E. sinensis in the Es14-3-3ζ-dsRNA group was significantly lower than that in the control group (Fig. 1I), suggesting that Es14-3-3ζ played a role in defending against S. eriocheiris infection. In summary, Es14-3-3ζ was involved in the process of S. eriocheiris infection and acted as a positive regulator in this process. The quantitative phosphoproteomic analysis of E. sinensis hemocytes revealed that during the early stage of S. eriocheiris infection, the phosphorylation levels of 36 proteins decreased by less than 0.5-fold, 562 proteins exhibited phosphorylation changes between 0.5- and 2-fold, and 10 proteins showed phosphorylation increased exceeding 2-fold (Fig. 2A). The phosphorylation level at the Y129 site of Es14-3-3ζ decreased to 0.192, representing the most significant phosphorylation change during S. eriocheiris infection (Fig. 2B and C). Meanwhile, Tyr129 (Y129) was highly conserved in different species from human to crabs (Fig. 2D). These experimental results indicate that Es14-3-3ζ was involved in the innate immune response following S. eriocheiris infection, with phosphorylation playing a critical regulatory role in this process. To assess the role of Es14-3-3ζ against S. eriocheiris infection, the S2 cells were transfected with pAc5.1-Es14-3-3ζ-GFP plasmid and infected with S. eriocheiris. The expression of pAc5.1-Es14-3-3ζ-GFP plasmid was confirmed by western blot (Fig. S2C). And subcellular localization shown 14-3-3ζ-GFP was localized at cytoplasm (Fig. S2D). The cellular morphology of S. eriocheiris+Es14-3-3ζ-GFP group was obviously better than S. eriocheiris + GFP group and S. eriocheiris group using inverted fluorescence microscopy (Fig. S2E). Meanwhile, according to the CCK-8 test, the cell viability of experimental group was dramatically higher than that of control group and blank group (Fig. S2F). qRT-PCR demonstrated that the S. eriocheiris load in the S. eriocheiris+Es14-3-3ζ-GFP group was significantly lower compared to S. eriocheiris + GFP group and S. eriocheiris group (Fig. S2G). S. eriocheiris invading into the S2 cells was observed by confocal laser scanning microscope as the red fluorescence. The results were the similar to the result of S. eriocheiris load (Fig. S2H). Based on the above data, the overexpression of Es14-3-3ζ decreased invasion of S. eriocheiris in S2 cells. Then to investigate the impact of Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation on S. eriocheiris infection, aspartic acid (Y129) was substituted for tyrosine (Y129D) to mimic the constitutive phosphorylation state of Es14-3-3ζ, while alanine was substituted for tyrosine (Y129A) to generate a mutant Es14-3-3ζ plasmid mimicking the phosphorylation-deficient condition (Fig. 3A). Fluorescence observation confirmed the overexpression of these mutants in S2 cells (Fig. S4A). As shown in Fig. 3B and C, compared to the GFP-transfected group, the apoptosis of S2 cells was significantly reduced in wild-type Es14-3-3ζ-GFP group. Moreover, the Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (Y129D) group exhibited a further significant decrease in apoptotic cells compared to both the GFP and wild-type Es14-3-3ζ-GFP groups. In contrast, apoptosis in the Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (Y129A) group showed no significant difference from the wild-type group. A similar result was also obtained from the CCK-8 assay (Fig. 3D). Cell viability was significantly higher in Es14-3-3ζ-GFP-transfected cells than in the GFP-transfected group. Additionally, cells transfected with Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (Y129D) showed significantly higher viability than both the GFP and wild-type Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (WT) groups, whereas Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (Y129A)-transfected cells exhibited no significant difference in viability compared to the wild-type group. Furthermore, the S. eriocheiris load in the Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (WT) group was much lower than in the GFP group. The S. eriocheiris load in the Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (Y129D) group was significantly reduced compared to both GFP and Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (WT) groups (Fig. 3D). In contrast, the Es14-3-3ζ-GFP (Y129A) group showed a slight increase in S. eriocheiris proliferation compared to the wild-type group but remained significantly lower than the GFP group (Fig. 3E). These findings suggest that Es14-3-3ζ aided host cells in resisting S. eriocheiris infection by enhancing cellular immunity. Additionally, the Y129D mutation, mimicking phosphorylation, enhances Es14-3-3ζ activity, stabilizing cell morphology and function, thereby further suppressing S. eriocheiris proliferation. After the pAC5.1-Es14-3-3ζ-GFP and pAC5.1-EsPI3K-FLAG plasmids were co-transfected into S2 cells, the interaction between Es14-3-3ζ and EsPI3K was detected using fluorescence microscopy and co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assays. As shown in Fig. S4B, fluorescence microscopy confirmed successful overexpression of EsPI3K in S2 cells transfected with the pAC5.1-EsPI3K-GFP plasmid. Meanwhile, the Co-IP assay in Fig. 5A demonstrated that Es14-3-3ζ and EsPI3K could interact in S2 cells. Cellular co-localization provided spatial evidence for the interaction between Es14-3-3ζ and EsPI3K. The extensive co-localization of Es14-3-3ζ and EsPI3K was observed in the cytoplasm of S2 cells (Fig. 5B) though laser confocal microscopy. As a key signaling protein, PI3K regulated cell proliferation, growth, and other processes through pathways. Did EsPI3K affect the phosphorylation of Es14-3-3ζ? As shown in Fig. 5C, after co-transfection of Es14-3-3ζ and EsPI3K, the phosphorylation level of Es14-3-3ζ increased to 2.51 compared to the control group though western blot. This result indicated that overexpressed EsPI3K could regulate the phosphorylation level of Es14-3-3ζ and acted as a kinase for Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation. To further support this conclusion, EsPI3K was interfered successfully using the dsRNA (the interference primers were shown in supplementary Table S1). As shown in Fig. 5D, compared to the control group, EsPI3K transcript levels were significantly decreased at 48 and 72 h post-injection though qRT-PCR. Then, the changes of Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation were also examined after EsPI3K RNAi. Western blot results showed that Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation decreased to 0.198 and 0.375 at 48 h and 72 h post-interference, respectively (Fig. 5E). The overexpression and RNAi experiments collectively established that EsPI3K regulated Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation. The EsPI3K transcript levels in hemocytes of E. sinensis after S. eriocheiris infection were also investigated though qRT-PCR. EsPI3K transcript levels increased after S. eriocheiris infection, with a trend consistent of the Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation level (Fig. 5F). In summary, EsPI3K could phosphorylate Es14-3-3ζ. These results demonstrated that Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation enhanced its interaction with antioxidant proteins, leading to reduced enzymatic activities of TRX1 and CAT. The inactivation of antioxidant proteins increased the ROS levels and aided the host in defending against S. eriocheiris infection. The 14-3-3 protein facilitates the folding of misfolded proteins to form inclusion bodies, thereby enabling the clearance of abnormal proteins, and serves as a pivotal component in protein interaction networks. Additionally, 14-3-3 directly interacts with various protein kinases, phosphatases, and other proteins to form novel complexes, subsequently participating in the transcriptional regulation and expression of downstream target genes [1]. 14-3-3 also plays a crucial role in the immune response of animals. Following infectious pancreatic necrosis virus (IPNV) infection in Salmo salar, significant induction of 14-3-3 transcription could be observed in host cell lines [2]. For instance, in Litopenaeus vannamei, the 14-3-3ε protein interacted with LvRab11, thereby exhibiting antiviral activity [3]. Additionally, in hematopoietic tissue cells of Cherax quadricarinatus, white spot syndrome virus (WSSV) infection altered the expression of proteins containing the 14-3-3, aiding the host in responding to viral infection [4]. The transcription of 14-3-3ζ in Penaeus monodon hemolymph was significantly upregulated after infection with Vibrio anguillarum or WSSV [5]. Similarly, Oncorhynchus mykiss also exhibited a significant increase in the transcription of 14-3-3ζ following pathogen infection [6]. Furthermore, 14-3-3 participates in immune processes by interacting with other proteins in aquatic invertebrates. For example, Macrobrachium rosenbergii 14-3-3 could bind to the WSSV envelope protein VP23 [7]. In summary, 14-3-3 plays an indispensable role in the defense mechanisms of aquatic invertebrates against pathogenic infections. But the regulation mechanism of 14-3-3 in animal immune was not clear until now. Protein phosphorylation, as one of the most common post-translational modifications, alters the physicochemical properties of proteins, protein-protein interactions [8], thereby regulating physiological and biochemical processes such as neural activity, cell proliferation, development, division, signal transduction [9–11]. 14-3-3 occupies a central role in signaling pathways through phosphorylation-dependent interactions, dynamically regulating plant growth, development, and stress response [12]. In the brains of Parkinson’s disease patients, phosphorylation of the 14-3-3θ at site S232 was significantly elevated and then weakened the activity of LRRK2 [13]. In plants, Arabidopsis thaliana 14-3-3λ and κ directly interacted with RLCKs and MAPKKKs, ultimately driving MAPK activation and enhancing disease resistance [14]. In apples, phosphorylation of 14-3-3 proteins reduced their ubiquitination-mediated degradation, increases melatonin levels, and strengthens resistance to pathogens [15]. Additionally, casein kinase-mediated phosphorylation of 14-3-3 modulated its interaction with Raf kinase, further influencing downstream signaling [12]. Similarly, in rice (Oryza sativa L.), the GF14b, a member of the 14-3-3 family, could be phosphorylated under qGL3 induction, enhancing its interaction with BZR1, and ultimately suppressed BZR1’s nuclear localization and stability [16–18]. In cancer, phosphorylation of specific 14-3-3 promoted cell proliferation or inhibit apoptosis [19]. While the role of 14-3-3 phosphorylation has been extensively studied in plants and higher animals, its underlying mechanisms in invertebrate remain poorly understood. Tremor disease (TD) caused by intracellular pathogen Spiroplasma eriocheiris is one of the most prevalent diseases in Chinese mitten crab, Eriocheir sinensis. In recent years, the incidence rate of TD has reached approximately 30%, with a mortality rate of nearly 100%, causing significant economic losses to the crab aquaculture [20]. S. eriocheiris is abundantly present in the muscles, gills, hemolymph, and other tissues of infected E. sinensis [21]. Infection with S. eriocheiris begins when the pathogen enters the E. sinensis through the gills, followed by its aggregation in target cells, namely hemocytes, where the bacteria form inclusion bodies and proliferate extensively. Subsequently, S. eriocheiris spreads via the circulatory system to various tissues, including connective and nervous tissues, leading to symptoms such as loss of appetite in the host. Eventually, infected crabs exhibit tremors in their appendages, ultimately leading to death [22]. Due to the lack of an adaptive immune system, E. sinensis can only rely on innate immune responses to defend against various pathogens [23, 24]. Therefore, a deeper understanding of the interaction between S. eriocheiris and the innate immunity of E. sinensis will shed light on the evolution of innate immunity. In this study, the positive role of 14-3-3ζ against S. eriocheiris was identified. This study represented the first report on the involvement of 14-3-3ζ phosphorylation-mediated oxidative stress in immune regulation in crustaceans. Furthermore, this study revealed that the kinase PI3K could promote the phosphorylation of 14-3-3ζ, thereby activating ROS levels to participate in immune responses. In summary, this research established a functional link between 14-3-3 phosphorylation and oxidative stress in crustaceans. These findings provided deeper insights into the innate immune system of invertebrates and established a novel theoretical foundation for the prevention and control of intracellular bacteria infection. Each bar represents the mean ± SD from three repeated experiments, comparisons between groups were analyzed by one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey multiple comparison tests (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001) In recent years, 14-3-3 proteins were proved to play a significant role in the immunity by regulating ROS levels. For instance, in the Pacific abalone (Haliotis discus hannai), both 14-3-3ζ and 14-3-3ε genes were significantly upregulated in response to Vibrio harveyi infection. Studies revealed that silencing 14-3-3ζ markedly suppressed ROS production and the expression of immune-related genes, whereas silencing 14-3-3ε had a minimal impact on ROS levels. This indicated that 14-3-3ζ played a crucial role in the positive regulation of ROS levels and innate immune responses [3]. Moreover, after infection with Taura syndrome virus (TSV) and WSSV, the transcription of 14-3-3 in Litopenaeus vannamei was significantly altered [34, 35]. Likewise, the transcription of 14-3-3ζ was significantly elevated in 12 kinds of cancer tissues, including colon adenocarcinoma, cholangiocarcinoma, among others [36]. This similarly indicated that 14-3-3 played a crucial role in the immune responses of both arthropods and mammals. In this study, after infection with S. eriocheiris, the expression of Es14-3-3ζ exhibited a trend of initial decrease followed by increase. And the knockdown of 14-3-3ζ via dsRNA led to a significant increase in copy number of S. eriocheiris in the E. sinensis hemocytes. Furthermore, targeted silencing of 14-3-3ζ by miRNAs resulted in a significant reduction of ROS levels in the E. sinensis hemocytes, which was ultimately associated with high mortality. Similarly, knockdown of 14-3-3ζ accelerated renal fibrosis and exacerbated acute kidney injury in a mouse model [37]. Therefore, Es14-3-3ζ participated in regulating the innate immune response by modulating ROS levels. The phosphoproteome revealed that during the early stages of S. eriocheiris infection, Y129 of Es14-3-3ζ was a phosphorylation site, and its phosphorylation level was significantly decreased. During S. eriocheiris infection, the phosphorylation at Y129 of Es14-3-3ζ showed an initial decrease followed by an increase. The phosphorylation modification of Es14-3-3ζ at Y129 played a critical role in this process. Protein phosphorylation, a reversible modification, can modulate protein activity and function, influence the cell cycle and proliferation, and participate in cellular signal transduction, ultimately contributing to immune regulation. This modification can either activate or inhibit the original protein’s activity. The overexpression of 14-3-3ζ in a mouse model slowed the process of renal fibrosis, reduced the expression of fibrosis-related proteins, and ultimately prevented acute kidney injury [37]. Similarly, the overexpression of wild-type Es14-3-3ζ (Es14-3-3ζ-WT), dephosphorylation-mimicking mutant (Es14-3-3ζ-Y129A), and the phosphorylation-mimicking mutant (Es14-3-3ζ-Y129D) significantly increased the viability of S2 cells, showing the most pronounced upregulation in the Es14-3-3ζ-Y129D group. Additionally, overexpression of Es14-3-3ζ-WT, Es14-3-3ζ-Y129A, and Es14-3-3ζ-Y129D significantly reduced S2 cell apoptosis and the copy number of S. eriocheiris, exhibiting the lowest levels of apoptosis and S. eriocheiris copies in the Es14-3-3ζ-Y129D group. These findings suggested that the phosphomimetic mutation activates the original activity of protein, aiding the host in resisting S. eriocheiris infection, thereby substantially enhancing cell viability. Furthermore, during Citrobacter rodentium infection of mouse CEC cells, AvrA increased Akt phosphorylation, leading to β-catenin phosphorylation and activation of its protein activity, which helped the host defend against pathogen invasion [38]. The phosphorylation of Es14-3-3ζ was involved in the immune response of crab, but the “switch” regulating this phosphorylation remains unknown. Proteins can interact with one or multiple binding partners, and these interactions are crucial for regulating various cellular processes [39]. Calcium-dependent protein kinases (CDPKs) were key kinases responsible for phosphorylating 14-3-3. Phosphorylated 14-3-3 enhanced its ability to function as an adaptor protein [40]. Akt1 kinase was a crucial serine/threonine kinase capable of phosphorylating the 14-3-3 binding motif (PC14-3-3), thereby regulating cardiomyocyte reprogramming. This phosphorylation event was essential for the functional role of 14-3-3 in cellular fate conversion [41]. Phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) was a critical signaling molecule involved in various cellular processes, including cell growth, survival, and metabolism. One of its key functions was the phosphorylation of downstream proteins, which modulated their activity and contributed to signal transduction. PI3K was well-known for its role in activating the protein kinase B (Akt) pathway. For instance, in T helper 17 (Th17) cells, PI3K-dependent activation of Akt occurred through the phosphorylation of Akt at specific sites, which was essential for cell activation, migration, and differentiation [42]. Similarly, in human spermatozoa, PI3K phosphorylated Akt to maintain cell viability, a process regulated by lysophosphatidic acid (LPA) signaling [43]. Moreover, PI3K activated the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway, which was involved in cell growth and metabolism. For example, in hepatic glycogen synthesis, PI3K phosphorylated mTOR to enhance downstream insulin signaling and promote glycogenesis [44]. In this study, the potential interacting partners of Es14-3-3ζ, and their interactions were successfully validated. PI3K, as a critical signal transduction protein, primarily functions to activate phosphorylation, thereby inhibiting apoptosis and promoting cell survival, ultimately maintaining tissue homeostasis [45]. During S. eriocheiris infection, EsPI3K initially decreased and then increased, a trend consistent with the phosphorylation level of Es14-3-3ζ. Furthermore, the overexpression of EsPI3K significantly increased the phosphorylation level of Es14-3-3ζ, while interference with EsPI3K led to a significant decrease of Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation. These results indicated there was a positive regulatory relationship between EsPI3K and the phosphorylation of Es14-3-3ζ. In summary, EsPI3K could phosphorylate Es14-3-3ζ. CAT and TRX1 are crucial antioxidant enzymes in cells, playing a vital role in protecting cells from oxidative stress damage. Therefore, they are of great significance in oxidative stress and disease treatment. In this study, 14-3-3ζ could interact with both CAT and TRX1. Moreover, the overexpression of wild-type Es14-3-3ζ(WT) and the phosphorylation-mimicking mutant (Y129D) significantly reduced the activity of TRX1 and CAT, whereas overexpression of dephosphorylation-mimicking mutant (Y129A) resulted in significantly increased activity of TRX1 and CAT. This demonstrated that the phosphomimetic form of Es14-3-3ζ enhanced its interaction with binding partners, leading to a decrease in TRX1 and CAT activity. Similar to our findings, the phosphorylation of OsGF14b strengthened its interaction with OsBZR1 [39]. Additionally, CDPK-phosphorylated 14-3-3 increased the binding to CDPKs and a greater tendency to interact with other phosphorylated proteins [40]. In addition, phosphorylation of the LC3-interacting region (LIR) of ATG8 enhanced its binding affinity to specific ATG8 family members, including LC3A, LC3C, GABARAP, and GABARAPL1. Phosphorylation by autophagy-related kinases was found to increase the strength of this interaction [46]. ROS are critical signaling molecules in cells and function through two pathways, one involves elevated ROS levels inducing pathogen death, while the other involves excessive ROS levels damaging the host’s defense system [47–49]. The overexpression of phosphorylation-mimicking mutant of Es14-3-3ζ (Y129D) significantly increased ROS levels in S2 cells. Combining enzyme activity and ROS level data, it could be concluded that Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation reduced enzyme activity correlates with decreased intracellular ROS levels. Similarly, under low-temperature stress, CAT enzyme activity in strawberry leaves initially increased and then decreased [50]. This suggested that early ROS accumulation helped activate antioxidant defenses, but as stress intensifies, the defense system failed to clear excess ROS, ultimately leading to cell damage or death. Moreover, TRX yielded comparable results. Inhibited TrxR1 was found to form pro-oxidant NADPH oxidase, which disrupted redox homeostasis and elevated ROS levels, thereby exacerbating oxidative stress [51]. These results indicated that phosphorylated Es14-3-3ζ enhanced its interaction with antioxidant proteins, leading to their inactivation, thereby positively regulating ROS levels and aiding the host in resisting S. eriocheiris infection. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of endogenous non-coding RNAs that bind to the 3’ untranslated region (UTR) of target mRNAs, leading to mRNA instability or impaired protein translation. In aquatic crustaceans, Marsupenaeus japonicus miR-1000 simultaneously targeted the WSSV viral genes WSV191 and WSV407, thereby inhibiting viral infection in shrimp [52]. Additionally, Apostichopus japonicus miR-92a agomir caused a decrease in 14-3-3ζ expression, along with a significant increase in apoptosis rate in coelomocytes [53]. The transfection of NUGC3 gastric cancer cells with miR-275 led to a significant downregulation of 14-3-3ζ and a marked increase in apoptotic cells [54]. In line with these findings, E. sinensis miR-2309-mediated silencing of Es14-3-3ζ resulted in a significant increase in the copy number of S. eriocheiris in hemocytes and a notable reduction in cell viability. Furthermore, targeted silencing of Es14-3-3ζ led to extensive fragmentation and death of crab hemocytes. Similarly, miR-21 regulated ROS levels by targeting PDCD4, ultimately influencing cardiac cell damage [55]. The miR-2309 silencing Es14-3-3ζ significantly reduced its phosphorylation and markedly decreased ROS levels in hemocytes. These findings provide new therapeutic insights for host defense against pathogen infection. In summary, EsPI3K phosphorylates Es14-3-3ζ, promoting its interaction with antioxidant proteins to regulate ROS levels. This process enhances cell viability, suppresses apoptosis, and ultimately helps the host defend against S. eriocheiris infection. This study represents the first report to elucidate the role of Es14-3-3ζ phosphorylation at a conserved tyrosine residue Tyr129. These findings not only provide novel insights for our understanding of innate immune mechanisms against intracellular pathogenic infections (Fig. 8).
